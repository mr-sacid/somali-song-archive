### What the song says

This is a song about a love that will not end and will not heal. It has lasted ten years. The singer keeps it from the people around her, telling them whatever will satisfy them, and all the while it has her buried to the knees. Just when it seemed to have passed, it has come back for a second time. Each part of the song ends on the same cry: *ribatayee Allahayow*, I have seized up, O my God.

### Ten years conceived and carried

The opening treats love as a pregnancy. It was conceived, *rimo*, and has been carried for ten years without ever being delivered. What comes out of it instead is a sound: *reen*, the deep, closed-mouth moan that rises from the chest. It is the noise of something in pain that has no words, and it leaves the one who carries it crippled.

### A raid that returns

The third stanza speaks in the language of war and of tracking. *Raad* is a footprint or trail, the thing a herder reads on the ground to find a lost animal or follow a raiding party. Here it is love that follows its own old trail back to her. *Rogaal* is a counter-raid, the second attack that comes when the first has been beaten off. She thought she had come through it once. This is the second wave.

### The load and the rope

The fourth stanza turns to the *awr*, the male camel that carries the household's loads. Somalis are by tradition nomadic herders, and every listener knew what loading a camel involves. The song says that it is not the weight of the load that wears the animal raw. It is the rope, when it works loose and shifts, that does the damage.

Read against the rest of the song, the load is the love itself, which she could bear. What harms her is everything around it that will not hold still.

### The thorn in the foot

The fifth stanza stays with the camel. *Ragaad* is the thorn or stone that lodges in a camel's foot. The song observes that the thorn is driven deepest into the very leg the animal puts its weight on. The more she leans on this love, the deeper it goes in.

### The body gives it away

The last part of the song is a list, each line ending in *-iyo*, "and", so that the items pile up without a pause: tears, heart, bosom, eyes, legs. Two of the lines use words that imitate a sound. *Ragax* is the sound of a sudden gush, here of tears. *Rug* is the sound of a heartbeat, and the song makes a verb of it for a heart that pounds.

She can lie to people, as the second stanza says, but her body cannot. The eyes go on watching for one person and the legs go on walking, and none of them gives up the dream.

### The sound of the song

Somali verse is held together by alliteration: the main words of every line begin with the same sound. This song alliterates on R from the title to the last line: *raaga*, *rimo*, *reemay*, *run*, *raali*, *ruug*, *raad*, *rogaal*, *ribatay*, *rar*, *raran*, *ruxmaa*, *ragaad*, *riix*, *roortay*, *rif*, *riiq*, *ragax*, *rug*, *rafaad*, *ruux*, *riyo*, *rubbad*, *raaxo*.

That is why so many of its words are rare ones. The poet needed a word in R for each thing he wanted to say, and reached into the vocabulary of herding and of war to find them.

### The singer

Magool was born Xaliimo Khaliif Cumar in Dhuusamareeb, in central Somalia, in 1948. She began singing as a girl with a small band in Mogadishu and then moved to Hargeysa, where a fellow musician, Yusuf Haji Adan, gave her the name Magool, "flower". In the 1970s she sang patriotic songs during the war over the Ogaden. Later in the decade she left the country, and stayed away until 1987. Her return concert in Mogadishu that year is reported to have drawn more than 160,000 people. She is known as *Hooyadii Fanka*, the Mother of Artistry. She died in Amsterdam in 2004.

### The poet

Xussein Aw Faarax, also written Xuseen Aw-Faarax Dubbad, was born in 1928 in the village of Bahal-Dhaman, near Hargeysa. He worked as a driver from 1942 and composed throughout. In the early 1950s he was among those who shaped the *heello*, the new kind of song from which modern Somali music grew. He is credited with the words of *Qolobaa Calankeed*, the song of the flag, set to music by Cabdillaahi Qarshe, which became the national anthem of Somalia in 2012. He wrote plays as well, among them *Cartan iyo Ceebla* (1956), and more than twenty songs over fifty years. Magool sang several of them. He died in Mogadishu in 1999.
