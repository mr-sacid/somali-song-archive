# About Kaydka Suugaanta

Kaydka Suugaanta is a small, slow archive of Somali songs and poems. Each entry shows the Somali text beside an English translation, with notes on the imagery, the difficult words, and the story behind the piece where I can find it.

The name means "the store of literature". A *kayd* is something put by and kept safe for later. *Suugaan* is literature, and for Somalis that has always meant, above all, poetry and song.

## Why I started this

My name is Mohamed. I'm a Somali from Garissa, Kenya, and I study civil engineering in Hungary. I love Somali poetry from the bottom of my heart.

So much of the beauty in these songs lives in things that don't survive a quick translation: the camel imagery, the alliteration, the words nobody uses in everyday speech anymore. I wanted a place where all of that is explained properly, for anyone who loves this poetry or is curious about the tradition, whether they grew up with the language or not.

This is a passion project. There are no ads, and I make no money from it. I add an entry when I have a free day, so it grows slowly.

## What is in an entry

- **The text**, in Somali and English side by side, stanza by stanza.
- **Recordings**, where they exist, so you can listen while you read.
- **Notes** on the imagery, the sound of the verse and the world it comes from.
- **Words**: the rare and old ones, explained.
- **Sources** for anything I say about a poet, a singer or the history.

## How the translations are made

I work from recordings and published texts. A first draft of each translation is made with the help of an AI tool. I then go through it line by line as a native speaker, correcting the meaning and supplying what a machine cannot know: what a word means to a herder, what a phrase implies, what the poet is pointing at. The notes are written the same way. The final wording is mine, and so are any mistakes.

I aim to stay close to the meaning and do not force rhyme or metre into English. The notes are there to explain what the translation cannot carry. Where a word is archaic, or a line can be read more than one way, I say so.

Somali spelling varies from one writer and region to another, and singers do not always pronounce a word the way a dictionary spells it. I keep each text as it is sung and written in my sources, so you may see spellings that differ from the standard.

I'm not a scholar of Somali literature. I'm a native speaker who loves this material and is learning as I go.

## Corrections are welcome

If you think a line is mistranslated, a word is misspelled, or a poet or singer is wrongly credited, please tell me. If you know the story behind a song, I would love to hear it. Much of this history lives in people's memories and nowhere else.

You can reach me at [mohaxcode@gmail.com](mailto:mohaxcode@gmail.com), or by [opening an issue on GitHub](https://github.com/mr-sacid/somali-song-archive/issues).

## Credits and rights

The songs and poems belong to their poets, composers and singers, and to their families. I share them here for education and preservation, with full credit. If you hold the rights to a work and want a credit corrected, or an entry changed or removed, write to me and I will do it promptly.

The recordings are embedded from YouTube and remain with the channels that published them.
